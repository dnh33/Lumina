# Worlds — Design Critique

The whole thing hinges on one problem: **the metaphor is named, not built.** Everything else follows from fixing that. Ranked below, most-impactful first.

## A. What to add

**1. Make the metaphor the layout grammar, not the paint.** Today all 13 worlds are the same skeleton — hero → anchors → timeline → modules → carousel → CTA — recolored and reworded. That's the entire "list with a coat of paint" problem in one sentence. Each metaphor should drive a structurally different primary interaction: *Constellation* (sci-fi/fantasy) = a pannable node-map where proximity encodes kinship and pulling a title lights up its lineage, not a scrolling grid; *Reading Room* (documentary) = a two-column dossier with a topic/argument table-of-contents on the left and curatorial prose with the credibility strip inline as citations; *Threshold* (horror/noir) = a darkened corridor that reveals one title at a time on approach; *Frontier* (western/travel) = a horizontal journey where the geo map IS the spine; *Panel* (anime/crime) = a real paneled composition where reading order and gutters carry meaning. *Risk:* six bespoke layouts is a large build and a11y surface — it only works if they're composed from a shared primitive kit, or you get six snowflakes.

**2. Turn the Companion into an ambient in-world guide instead of an exit door.** Right now it's a CTA that ejects the user to `/chat` at the exact moment engagement peaks, and the global ChatDock already floats awkwardly over `/genre`. Convert it into a diegetic narrator that speaks in the world's `register` (the lexicon + tonePrompt already exist and currently do almost nothing): it annotates as you move, offers to re-steer ("more dread, less gore"), and answers "what should I watch first" without leaving. *Risk:* chattiness kills immersion — it must be pull, silent by default, never interrupting.

**3. Put "why this belongs here" provenance on every card.** The server already computes anchor matches, shared directors, topics, and credibility signals — surface them. On expand, a card explains its own placement: "matched your anchor *Blade Runner*," "shares director," "topic: surveillance." This is the *private-app superpower*: no black-box engagement ranking, full transparency into a taste model that's genuinely the user's. It also teaches people their own taste. *Risk:* exposing weak matches ("because you rated this 2 stars") needs graceful phrasing.

**4. Give a world persistence — save, resume, deep-link.** A world currently re-renders stateless on every visit; nothing is remembered. It should persist scrub position, steer settings, dismissed items, and progress through the argument/watch-order, and support deep-links like `/genre/noir?decade=1950s&mood=paranoia`. Local-first single-user is the whole reason this matters: the world should be an ownable *place* you return to, not a feed that refreshes. *Risk:* stale state when the library changes — needs a quiet reconcile.

**5. Wire sound and motion to the metaphor via the existing `cueBeatMap`.** Each register already declares beat cues and `playCue` exists, yet no sound fires. Give each world a sonic/motion signature — Reading Room: paper and quiet; Threshold: a low drone and doors; Constellation: drifting chimes; Warm Interior: vinyl crackle — triggered on meaningful beats (crossing a threshold, pulling a star, turning a page). Near-zero UI cost, huge differentiation. *Risk:* sound fatigue — sparse, sound-off default, reduced-motion honored.

**6. Replace the stack of parallel rails with one spatial spine per world.** The page is a dashboard of independent widgets (timeline grid, topic cluster, credibility, watch-order, carousel) with no causal link between them. Pick ONE primary axis you travel along per world — time for docs, map for frontier, node-graph for constellation — and demote the other modules to detail that surfaces contextually as you move. *Risk:* less at-a-glance breadth; some users want the buffet, so keep a "show everything" escape hatch.

**7. Add a per-world "deeper in" serendipity gesture.** The app's premise is depth, not breadth — so reward it with a deliberate act that steps away from comfort: "jump to an adjacent star" (a title two hops from your taste) in Constellation, "open the next door" (escalating intensity) in Threshold, "ride further out" in Frontier. Calibrate distance using the ignored/anchor/rating signals you already have. *Risk:* it must be a chosen gesture, never an auto-hijack of the main view.

**8. Let the world visibly reflect your library's density.** Watched / watchlist / ignored already exist as signals — render them as *place*: a constellation with lit vs. dark stars, a reading room with read vs. unread spines, a frontier map with explored vs. unexplored territory. It turns the taste graph into a sense of progress and gap without any gamified engagement-bait. *Risk:* completionism creep — frame it as texture, not a score.

## B. Blind angles

**1. The metaphor is asserted, never expressed.** The entire differentiation budget is spent on an eyebrow string and a gradient; Reading Room and Threshold share a skeleton. This is the root cause of every "coat of paint" complaint — fix the layout grammar (A1) and half of B dissolves.

**2. It's a dashboard, not a journey.** Hero → anchors → timeline → modules → carousel → CTA is a vertical stack of unrelated widgets with no through-line — no reason section 4 follows section 3. Immersion requires a spine and cause-and-effect; this has a scroll.

**3. Interaction dead-ends undercut the affordances.** The TimelineScrubber *looks* page-wide but filters only its own little grid — a control that lies about its scope is a broken affordance. The Companion CTA ejects the user out of the world at peak engagement. And any AnchorFrame chip that's a label rather than a link is a verb the user reaches for and doesn't find.

**4. The `register` data is rich and nearly unused.** lexicon, tonePrompt, and cueBeatMap are all defined per world, but the UI expresses a single tone line in the hero — no sound uses the beat map, the Companion doesn't speak in-register, module copy isn't relexified. Personality is declared in config and withheld from the screen.

**5. Empty, thin, and error states will break the metaphor, not just the layout.** A single-user library *will* have sparse genres — a "Constellation" of three titles isn't a constellation, a "Reading Room" with no arguments is an empty shelf, and the `Generic` fallback metaphor is the system admitting it has nothing to say. The niche-gate exists, but the real question is whether the metaphor *degrades gracefully* or collapses into a husk — and the same applies mid-load or when the LLM `argument`/enrichment fails. Immersion dies at the seams first.

**6. There's no place, memory, or return — the design imports feed-app statelessness into a context that wants the opposite.** A social app optimizes for a fresh feed; a private, single-user catalog should optimize for a stable, ownable, returnable *place*. Because every visit re-renders from scratch with no persisted state, deep-link, or personal history, the one thing the local-first premise makes possible — a world that's genuinely yours over time — is the one thing the current design forecloses.
